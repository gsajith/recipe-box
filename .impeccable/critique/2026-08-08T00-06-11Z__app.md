## Overall Impression

The craft here is real and unevenly distributed. The visual system is committed and specific, the capture path is genuinely excellent product thinking, and the thumbnail loading engineering is careful in a way most teams skip. Then the interaction layer underneath it is missing: you cannot open a recipe without a mouse, every card carries an invisible tappable delete button, and the two surfaces that matter most on a phone are majority chrome.

**The single biggest opportunity:** the product is optimized for *saving* and neglected for *using*. Capture is polished to a shine; the moment a cook opens a recipe at arm's length with wet hands — the scene you named as primary — the interface hands them 15 tag controls and hides the actual recipe behind a 12.8px link with a 96×19px tap target.

## What's Working

**1. The capture path is excellent product design.** Clipboard detection on both `visibilitychange` and `window.focus` with a 50ms debounce, a live preview that resolves either a share token or an OG scrape, dedupe against saved URLs, and a share-target that fires the save **on mount with no confirmation and auto-returns in 1500ms**. That last call is right and most teams would get it wrong — PRODUCT.md says capture is an interruption, and this respects it literally.

**2. The recipe card grid, and the engineering under it.** The thumbnail skeleton is painted as the `<img>`'s own background so it needs no wrapper and can't disturb the five containers these live in, with a distinct `data-state="error"` that stops the pulse, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honored. Measured across 84 real recipes: **0 broken images**. Careful work in service of a stated principle.

**3. The Inversion Rule, actually executed.** Expanding the profile deck drops a full search + filter + grid onto terracotta and every control inverts into white-alpha tiers rather than switching palettes. Most systems declare a rule like this and break it the first time a component lands on a colored ground. Related: `FilterDropdown` is the best-built control in the product — 4 options, portalled, correct `role="listbox"`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-haspopup`.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Every recipe card carries an invisible, tappable delete button

**What.** `.deleteBtn` (`RecipeList.module.css:192`) computes `opacity:0`, `pointer-events:auto`, `visibility:visible`, 40×38px, pinned top-right on all 84 cards. `document.elementFromPoint` at its centre returns the button. It is revealed only by `.recipeCard:hover` — which does not exist on touch, the confirmed primary platform. There is no `:focus-within` variant. The armed confirm state (the "Delete?" label and the `deletePulse` animation) **also renders at opacity 0**.

**Why it matters.** On a phone the top-right corner of a card is exactly where a thumb lands when grabbing to scroll. Two taps there inside the 3-second window destroys a recipe permanently — no visible warning, no visible confirm, no undo, no toast. Both assessments landed on this independently; it is the only finding either called P0 on its own.

**Fix.** `pointer-events: none` at rest, restored by the hover rule. Gate the whole reveal behind `@media (hover: hover)`. On touch, drop delete from the card entirely — it already exists in the detail modal, correctly visible as a blurred pill. Force the confirming state to `opacity: 1` unconditionally. Add `aria-label="Delete recipe"`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] Opening a recipe requires a mouse

**What.** `recipeCard` is a bare `<div onClick>` (`RecipeCardView.tsx:24`): `role: null`, `tabindex: null`, not focusable. Same for `/users` cards (`<li onClick>`), notification rows, and the profile's expandable deck. Measured: **105 tab stops on `/`, zero of which open a recipe** — 84 are the invisible delete buttons. Across five surfaces: **0 `<main>`, 0 `<nav>`, and `<h1>` present on only two routes** (one of them empty). 85 interactive `div`/`li` elements on `/` carry an onClick with no role or tabindex.

The detail modal is not a dialog: `role`, `aria-modal`, `aria-labelledby`, `tabindex` all `null`; **0 elements marked `inert`**; 70 Tab presses with it open landed **0 stops inside it**; Escape pressed 3× left it open; body scroll was not locked (`overflow: hidden auto`) and the page scrolled behind it.

**Why it matters.** Opening a recipe *is* the product, and it is unreachable for keyboard and screen-reader users on every surface. Straightforward WCAG 2.1.1 failure on a product PRODUCT.md positions for public growth against Paprika and Cookpad.

**Fix.** Convert the four interactive `div`/`li` wrappers to `<button type="button">`. Add `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal` + focus trap + Escape + `inert` background + body scroll lock to both modals. Add one `<h1>` per route and a `<main>` landmark in `app/(app)/layout.tsx`. Fill the empty `<h1>` on `/users`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] Chrome outranks the recipe on both primary surfaces

**What.** Two instances of one problem.

*Home, at 390×844:* the first recipe card starts at **y=444 — 52.6% of the fold is chrome**. Tapping "…show all tags" expands 8 chips to **39, 523px tall**, pushing the first card to **y=815 (96.6% chrome, zero recipes visible)** — and there is no way back, because `setAllTagsShown(true)` is never inverted and no "show less" exists in the DOM. 39 frequency-sorted options at one decision point is ~10× working memory.

*Detail modal, at 390px:* reading content occupies **218px**; the tag editor occupies **15 controls**. Three of them (`lunch`, `dinner`, `medium`) render **twice** as pixel-identical green pills ~200px apart, and tapping the upper copy is a **dead no-op** (`handleAddSuggestedTag` has no else branch, `RecipeDetail.tsx:117`). The only route to the actual recipe is `youtube.com` at **12.8px, 96×19px**. The modal contains **zero terracotta elements** — the One Door Rule isn't violated, it's unfulfilled. There is no door.

**Why it matters.** This is the kitchen scene: arm's length, busy hands, glancing. It presents a 15-control editor and hides the one thing the cook is there for. The duplicated dead chips actively teach users the interface is broken.

**Fix.** Add a full-width terracotta **"Open recipe"** button under the title; collapse the tag editor behind the pencil icon that already exists for metadata. Make suggestion chips true toggles. On home, add "show fewer" and move tags behind a fourth `FilterDropdown` — that pattern is already built, already sized right, already accessible. Target: first recipe above y=300 on a 390px phone.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

**What.** Measured, alpha-composited:

Focus: **4 text inputs have no focus indicator at all** (outline `none`, box-shadow unchanged) despite `--ring-focus` existing as a token for exactly this. Rings that do appear are inconsistent — Chrome's default blue on some elements, `3px #6B6055` on the bell, `3px #1A1A1A` on menu triggers. And `a.logo` computes to **128.9 × 0px** on every route (`AppHeader.module.css:19` sets `line-height: 0` on a block anchor), so the wordmark's hit box and focus ring have zero height — the same root cause as the `text-occlusion` flag on `/settings`, where the `em` overhangs the avatar button by 49px.

Separately: **18 of 20 interactive elements on `/` are under 44×44** (all tag chips 33px, all dropdown triggers 33px), **37 of 40 in the modal** (tag remove buttons are **14×17.6**), **5 of 5 on `/users`** (Follow is 28.2px tall). PRODUCT.md explicitly says the kitchen scene sets a floor that *exceeds* the usual minimums.

**Why it matters.** The terracotta failure is the sharpest one: DESIGN.md makes that color the single door on every screen, and its label is below AA. This is a system-level fix, not a page-level one — and it lands in DESIGN.md, not just the CSS.

**Fix.** Darken the terracotta *text* pairing (`--orange-dark #A14F34` gives 5.1:1) or raise button labels to large-text size, then record the decision in DESIGN.md. Raise `--border` for meaningful boundaries or accept it explicitly as decorative and stop relying on it as "the form". Replace the `·` separators with a non-text divider. Apply `var(--ring-focus)` to a global `:focus-visible`. Fix `line-height: 0` on the logo. Pad chips and triggers to a 44px hit area while keeping the 33px pill visual.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] Failure states lie, dead-end, or say nothing

**What.** Four failures, all verified in source or live:

1. **The false empty state.** Search `zzzqqq` against 84 recipes → **"No recipes saved yet. Add your first recipe!"** `RecipeList` only knows `recipes.length === 0` and has no notion of an active filter. No "clear search", and the tag box vanishes so the filtering cue disappears with it.
2. **The silent clipboard failure.** `setClipboardUrl(null)` fires *before* `await handleAddRecipe`, which throws on `!response.ok` with no catch at the call site. Banner vanishes, spinner ends, nothing saved, nothing said. A 500 from `/api/recipes` was observed live.
3. **The share-target dead end.** The raw server string `"Failed to extract recipe metadata from URL"` plus a single ghost "Go to RecipeBox" that **discards the URL**. The same button is a primary terracotta pill in the duplicate state and a ghost pill in the error state. PRODUCT.md Principle 4 calls degraded metadata first-class; here degraded extraction produces no save at all.
4. **`alert()`** for profile save failures, in a system that specifies a chili-red error banner.

Plus `new URL(recipe.url).hostname` (`RecipeDetail.tsx:332`) is unguarded and throws during render on a malformed URL — the only URL parse in the app without a try/catch.

**Fix.** Pass `hasActiveFilters || searchQuery` into `RecipeList` for a distinct "No recipes match — Clear filters" state. Wrap the clipboard save in try/catch and keep the banner up with an inline retry. On share-target failure offer **"Save the link anyway"** — hostname title, no thumbnail, exactly the degraded state the design system already handles. Replace both `alert()`s. Guard the `new URL`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## Minor Observations

- `/settings` has no `<h1>` — you navigate to "Settings" and the page never says "Settings". `/users`' `<h1>` is empty.
- The Settings Save button breaks three DESIGN.md rules at once: green instead of terracotta, 8px radius instead of a pill, full-strength green disabled state instead of oat.

- Filter chip drift: dropdown triggers are oat, tag chips are transparent — two resting states for one job, 20px apart.
- The notification panel is titled "Following" but interleaves "@gs3 started following you" (about you) with recipe saves (about them) under one badge. Unread state is `localStorage`-only, so it doesn't sync across devices, and when the key is absent the count defaults to *every item in the feed*.

- List view renders *all* tags per row while grid caps at 2, so the dense scanning view is dominated by chips and often omits cook time and servings — it carries less scannable information than the grid.
- 110px square thumbnail crops mangle YouTube title cards ("ARNITAS TACO", "NE POT RIGATO") in list view and the profile fan.
- The onboarding gate is social: a new user's mandatory first action is choosing a public handle, display name, and avatar, with no skip — before saving a single recipe. PRODUCT.md Principle 5 says the collection is personal and the graph is optional.

- The Add Recipe modal has no paste button and no clipboard prefill, despite the page behind it running full clipboard detection.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is honored only by `RecipeThumbnail` — the staggered fan, panel slide, banner entrance, delete pulse, and expand transition all ignore it.
- `/users` images are eager-loaded (0 of 5 lazy); `/settings` likewise.

## Questions to Consider

1. **What is the detail modal actually for?** If it's the kitchen surface it needs one terracotta "Open recipe" button and little else. If it's the tagging surface it shouldn't be what a tap on a card produces. Today it's both, and the 12.8px source link is losing.
2. **Why does the same card do two different things** — modal on `/`, leave-the-app on a profile? Which is right, and should the other change, or should profile cards look different so the difference is legible?
3. **Is a 39-chip wall the right answer to 84 recipes, or the wrong question?** The three dropdowns you already built are the best control in the product. Why does the largest, messiest dimension get the worst pattern?
4. **What should happen when extraction fails?** PRODUCT.md says degraded metadata is first-class; the code refuses to save at all. Is a bare link with a hostname title genuinely worse than nothing?
5. **Does a new user need a username before they need a recipe?** What would activation look like if the first screen after sign-in were a paste field?
6. **What is the bell counting?** It mixes follows with saves under one number, and you can't save anything from it. If the social graph is the moat, this is the moat's only surface — and it's a read-only list that sends people to other websites.
7. **What would a confident version of the phone home screen look like?** Today: 444px of chrome, then recipes.

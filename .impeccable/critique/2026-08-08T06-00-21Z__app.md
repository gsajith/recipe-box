## Design Specificity Verdict

**The app is authored. The landing page is a template. They do not look like they were made by the same person — and the landing page is the only one a stranger sees.**

Inside the product, specificity improved. The detail modal at 390 — full-bleed photograph, smoked-glass Back pill, Playfair title, one terracotta "Open recipe" — is a surface no template produces. The profile at 1440 is the best work in the repo.

The landing page inverts it. The hero type is genuinely yours: 80px Playfair 800 at −2px tracking, third line italic in butter yellow. Everything below it is the category's default page.

The single most damning measurement: **`<img>` count is 0 across the entire landing page**, at both viewports — 0 `<picture>`, 0 `<video>`, 3 inline SVGs, and exactly one background image (the dot screen). A product whose promise is "we fetch the title, photo, and source automatically" markets itself with **zero photographs**, while the app three clicks away is full of them.

### Not slop — earned, and worth defending

The app is remarkably clean of the surface-habit list, and that is not luck. ## Two findings the assessments disagreed on — both resolved, both mine

**1. The "84 recipes saved" headline is completely occluded at 390.** A said occluded; B measured it as not clipped. Both were right about different failures: it is **not** clipped by the container (242×48, fully inside), it is **painted over** — `elementFromPoint` returns `.profileCard` at all five probe points across the label. The cause is the `z-index: 2` I added to `.topRowContainer` in `b24a261` to fix the *stats* being painted over. I moved the occlusion from one element to another and shipped it. **This is a regression I introduced while fixing the last critique.**

**2. The terracotta AA exception does not cover the landing CTAs.** I recorded it in DESIGN.md as acceptable because 3.69:1 clears the **3:1 large-text** bar. Measured: both landing CTAs are **15.2px at weight 700**, which is not large text (needs ≥18.66px bold). So they fail 4.5:1 with no exception covering them. The rider I wrote — "prefer large/bold label type where the layout allows" — is exactly what the landing page doesn't do. The modal's "Open recipe" at 19.2px/700 *does* qualify. The exception is sound; its application on the landing page is not.

## Overall Impression

The interaction layer got genuinely better — 17 to 25, with every point earned at the root. What the slop lens exposes is that the problem was never evenly distributed: the app was authored and under-built, while the landing page was never authored at all. It is the one surface where nobody made a decision, and it is the only surface a prospect sees before committing to Google OAuth.

**The single biggest opportunity:** the landing page is selling a product it refuses to show. You have 84 recipes with beautiful photography, a card component already built, and an extraction pipeline that is the actual differentiator. The page shows a dot pattern and three lucide icons.

## What's Working

**1. The detail modal fixed the design problem, not just the accessibility problem.** The easy version of that ticket adds `role="dialog"` and stops. This one did that *and* re-answered the question underneath: tag editing demoted behind Edit, notes in an oat sub-panel, one full-width terracotta door. 40 interactive controls at rest down to 6.

**2. The share-target failure ladder.** "We couldn't read the recipe details" → **Save the link anyway** → Try again → home. Most products treat extraction failure as a dead end; this treats it as PRODUCT.md Principle 4 says to, and preserves the user's work at the moment they're most likely to abandon.

**3. Repo-wide restraint on the slop list.** Zero gradient text, zero hard offset shadows, zero monospace costume, zero emoji-as-icons in the app, every blur justified. Most codebases accumulate three or four of these by default.

### [P2] The tag system: noise on cards, a 40-item scroller in the filter
Cards show `tags[0]` and `tags[1]` **by array order** (`RecipeItemContent.tsx:107`). The filter that would rescue this is 40 alphabetical options in a 360px scroller with no search input.

**Fix:** rank card tags by *inverse* corpus frequency — show the two rarest tags per recipe, a one-line sort against the frequency map already computed. Add a type-to-filter input at the top of the dropdown and pin the 5 most-used tags above the alphabetical list.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`
